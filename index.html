Rest of Europe 1946–Present

A narrative-research knowledge base on continental European history from the end of the Second World War and the postwar settlement through the Cold War division of the continent, decolonisation, the collapse of the communist states, and the reordering of Europe to the present day. The United Kingdom is treated separately in its own wiki. Pages are grounded in the deposited source corpus — French (de Gaulle’s Discours et messages, Zancarini-Fournel, Bantigny), Spanish (Tusell), German (Winkler, Jäger & Link, Weber’s GDR documents, Kowalczuk), European-integration (Moravcsik, the Cambridge History of the EU), Italian (Mammarella, Gentiloni Silveri, Perrone on Mattei), and Soviet/Russian (the История СССР, История КПСС, Gromyko-Ponomarev, Pikhoya, Yurchak, Medinsky-Torkunov).

Narrative arc

The postwar settlement and the Cold War division of Europe opened with reconstruction and the hardening of two blocs — the iron curtain and the cold-war confrontation of world-socialist-system and the West. France rebuilt under the weak fourth-republic while de Gaulle waited through his traversée du désert, then returned in may-1958-crisis to found the fifth-republic, settle the algerian-war-1954-62, and pursue a policy of national independence. Germany was divided into two states, the Federal Republic under Adenauer’s Westintegration and the GDR under the SED, whose Stalinization climaxed in east-german-uprising-1953 and the building-berlin-wall-1961. Italy became a republic and, under centrismo and the DC, anchored itself in the Atlantic alliance. Spain stayed frozen in francoism until the dictator’s death.

The two Germanies and the era of détente saw the FRG’s sozialmarktwirtschaft and wirtschaftswunder, Brandt’s new-ostpolitik-1969-73, and the Schmidt years (the german-autumn-1977) even as the GDR drifted toward crisis. European integration advanced from the treaty-of-rome-1957 through the european-monetary-system-1979, the single-european-act-1986, and the maastricht-treaty-1992 toward the euro. France passed from the trente glorieuses through may-1968 to the Mitterrand presidency and cohabitation. Italy moved from centro-sinistra through the anni di piombo — the moro-kidnapping-1978 — to the Tangentopoli collapse. Spain made the Transition to democracy.

The end of the USSR and the reordering of Europe followed from the stagnation of the Brezhnev era and the perestroika-1985-91 of Gorbachev — the chernobyl-1986, the august-putsch-1991, and the dissolution-ussr-1991. The fall-of-berlin-wall-1989 and german-reunification-1990 ended the division of Germany; the eastern eastern-enlargement-2004 and the euro-crisis-2010 reshaped the Union. The Russian Federation under Yeltsin moved through the russian-constitutional-crisis-1993 and russian-default-1998 to the consolidation of the vertical-of-power under vladimir-putin.

How to read

  1. Start with the major events (postwar reconstruction, 1956, 1961, 1968, 1975–78, 1989–91, 1992).
  2. Read entities as continuous political biographies tied to those crises.
  3. Use concepts for structures that span decades (cold-war, fifth-republic, ostpolitik, european-integration, centrismo, developed-socialism).
  4. Every page ends with a Sources section pointing at the underlying books.
  5. Native-language glosses follow the house conventions.
