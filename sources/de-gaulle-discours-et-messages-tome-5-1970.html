Discours et Messages, Tome 5 : Vers le terme (1966-1969)

Charles de Gaulle — Discours et messages, Tome V : Vers le terme (Janvier 1966–Avril 1969). Librairie Plon, Paris, 1970; the present digital edition Plon / Édi8, 2014. The fifth and last of the five-tome collected edition of the speeches, messages and press conferences of General de Gaulle from 18 June 1940 to 28 April 1969, edited with the assistance of François Goguel; this volume covers the last years of his presidency, ending with the referendum of April 1969 and his retirement.

AuthorCharles de Gaulle (1890–1970) — French soldier and statesman, founder and first President of the Fifth Republic (1959–1969)
EditionLibrairie Plon, 1970; digital edition Plon / Édi8, 2014; text established with the collaboration of M. François Goguel
StructureNote de l’éditeur + speeches, messages and press conferences arranged chronologically by year (1966–1969); 16 files in the EPUB, ~5326 lines / ~950,000 characters of extracted text
PeriodJanuary 1966 to April 1969 — the years “towards the term”: the second presidential mandate, the policy of national independence (the departure from the integrated command of NATO, the Moscow visit of 1966), the “live Quebec” affair of 1967, the Polish, Romanian and Turkish visits, the crisis of May 1968, and the retirement of General de Gaulle after the referendum of April 1969
NoteA contemporary collection of de Gaulle’s own texts, not a narrative history — the exact texts of his determined speeches, his hand-written broadcast and televised allocations, and his official addresses and press conferences. It is the closing volume of the series and ends the record of his public life on 28 April 1969. For this wiki it supplies, from the President’s own voice, the French case for the years 1966–1969

Contents. A chronological record of de Gaulle’s statements from January 1966 to April 1969. The volume opens with the texts of 1966 — the year of the Conférence de presse of 21 February 1966 on the reform of the alliance and the departure from the integrated command of NATO, the addresses to the Foire Internationale de Lille, to the Congrès international de la responsabilité médicale at Versailles, to Douaumont for the fiftieth anniversary of Verdun, and to the Institut de France for the tricentenary of the Académie des Sciences, and then the great state visit to the Soviet Union of June–July 1966 (the allocations at Moscow, the Mossoviet, the University of Moscow, Academgorod, Leningrad, Kiev, Volgograd, the military exercise, and the broadcast to the Soviet radio and television), the reception of the King of Laos, the tour of Djibouti, and the Pacific tour of September 1966 (Phnom-Penh, Nouméa, Papeete, the Polynesian Territorial Assembly, Pirae and Taravao in Tahiti). The year 1967 carries the Vœux aux Armées, the visit to the Vatican, to the French ecclesiastical colony of Rome and to Venice, the reception of the President of Turkey, the North American visit of July 1967 (Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon, the Anse au Foulon, and the balcony of the Hôtel de Ville of Montreal with the celebrated closing to the “live Quebec” — Vive le Québec libre!), the Polish visit of September 1967 (Cracow, the Diète polonaise, and the broadcast at Wilanów), the reception of Ayub Khan, and the visit to the Cour de Cassation. The year 1968 carries the Romanian visit of May 1968 (the discourse before the Grande Assemblée nationale, the University of Bucharest, the Romanian radio and television), the crisis of May 1968 and the broadcast allocations at its height, the Conférence de presse of June 1968, the dissolution of the National Assembly, the closing of the events, the Turkish visit of October 1968 (the allocation at the Lycée Galatasaray of Istanbul, the Turkish radio and television), and the allocation at the Invalides. The year 1969 brings the final texts — the Vœux aux Armées, the Conférence de presse of January 1969, the allocations at Quimper and at the reception of President Bokassa of the Central African Republic, the reception of President Richard Nixon, the allocation radiodiffusée et télévisée on the referendum of 27 April 1969 on the reform of the Senate and the regionalisation, and the final text of 28 April 1969 announcing his retirement. The volume ends with the Notes (383 numbered notes) prepared by François Goguel.

Character of the source. A first-person record of the President of the Fifth Republic’s own words, kept as exact texts for the determined occasions. Its character is that of acts of government and declarations of policy — on national independence and the reform of the Atlantic alliance (the departure from the integrated command in 1966), on the rapprochement with the Soviet Union and with the states of eastern Europe, on the relationship with the French of Quebec, on the crisis of May 1968, and on the closing reform projects of the presidency. Goguel’s aide-mémoire and notes situate each text in its circumstances.

Quality. Good. Born-digital EPUB of the Plon/Édi8 edition, extracted via OPF spine order with page markers. French text clean and complete; the 383 numbered notes are present at the end. A few typographical artefacts from the source typesetting noted (e.g. “PRONCONCÉE” for “prononcée,” “FLOSSE” for “Floess” in the Pirae text); no OCR errors, the text being digital in origin.

1966

National independence, the departure from the integrated command, and the great eastern and Pacific tours.

  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 21 February 1966 — on the reform of the Atlantic alliance and the withdrawal of France from the integrated command of NATO.
  • The allocation lors de l’inauguration de la Foire internationale de Lille; the allocation à Versailles au Congrès international de la responsabilité médicale.
  • The allocation à Douaumont pour le cinquantième anniversaire de la bataille de Verdun.
  • The allocation à l’Institut de France à l’occasion du tricentenaire de l’Académie des Sciences.
  • The state visit to the Soviet Union of June–July 1966: the allocations at the Mossoviet, at the University of Moscow, at Academgorod (Novosibirsk), at Leningrad, at Kiev, at Volgograd, at the military exercise in the suburbs of Moscow, the allocation at Moscow, and the broadcast to the Soviet radio and television.
  • The reception of King Savang Vatthana of Laos; the allocation à Djibouti devant l’Assemblée territoriale de la Côte française des Somalis.
  • The Pacific tour of September 1966: the allocation at Phnom-Penh to the popular meeting, at Nouméa, at Papeete (place Joffre), before the Assemblée territoriale de la Polynésie française, at Pirae (Tahiti) in reply to the speech of the mayor Gaston Flosse, and at Taravao (Tahiti) in reply to the discourse of the doyen of the district chiefs.
  • The allocation à la Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the reception of King Mahendra of Nepal.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 28 October 1966; the allocation à l’occasion du XXe anniversaire de l’U.N.E.S.C.O.; the allocation lors de l’inauguration de l’usine marémotrice de la Rance; the reception of M. Kossyguine, President of the Council of Ministers of the U.S.S.R.

1967

The Vatican, the “live Quebec” affair, and the Polish visit.

  • The Vœux aux Armées and the allocation lors de la présentation des vœux des Corps constitués.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 16 May 1967.
  • The visit to the Vatican: the allocation au Vatican and the allocation devant la colonie ecclésiastique française de Rome; the allocation à Venise.
  • The reception of President Sunay of Turkey.
  • The North American visit of July 1967: the allocations at Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon and at the Anse au Foulon; the allocation au balcon de l’Hôtel de Ville de Montréal — the address to the crowd on the balcony, closing with “Vive le Québec libre!” — and the allocation à l’Université de Montréal.
  • The Polish visit of September 1967: the allocation à l’Université de Cracovie, the discours devant la Diète polonaise, and the allocation radiodiffusée et télévisée à Varsovie au palais de Wilanów.
  • The reception of Marshal Ayub Khan, President of Pakistan; the allocation à la Cour de Cassation; the reception of M. Soglo, President of Dahomey.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 27 November 1967; the Vœux aux Armées and the closing allocations of the year.

1968

The Romanian visit, the crisis of May 1968, and the dissolution of the Assembly.

  • The reception of General Abdul Rahman Mohammad Aref, President of Iraq; the allocation à la mémoire des officiers et marins du sous-marin « Minerve »; the allocation à l’ouverture de la 50e Foire internationale de Lyon.
  • The Romanian visit of May 1968: the discours à la Grande Assemblée nationale de la République socialiste de Roumanie, the discours à l’Université de Bucarest, and the allocation à la radiodiffusion et à la télévision roumaines.
  • The crisis of May 1968: the broadcast allocations at the height of the events of May–June 1968.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 7 June 1968 — on the dissolution of the National Assembly and the “participation.”
  • The allocation à la Butte Chaumont.
  • The Turkish visit of October 1968: the allocation à Istanbul au Lycée Galatasaray, the allocation à la radiodiffusion et à la télévision turques.
  • The allocation aux Invalides; the Vœux aux Armées and the closing allocations of the year.

1969

The final texts and the retirement of General de Gaulle.

  • The allocation à Quimper.
  • The reception of President Bokassa of the Central African Republic; the reception of President Richard Nixon of the United States.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 16 January 1969.
  • The allocation radiodiffusée et télévisée on the reform of the Senate and the regionalisation.
  • The referendum of 27 April 1969 and its rejection.
  • The final allocation radiodiffusée et télévisée of 28 April 1969 announcing the retirement of General de Gaulle — the last text of the series.

Notes

The volume closes with 383 numbered notes (prepared by François Goguel) identifying persons, places, dates and events referred to in the texts — the details of the reform of the alliance in 1966, the circumstances of the Moscow visit, the reception at Montreal of July 1967, the events of May 1968, and the closing referendum of April 1969.
