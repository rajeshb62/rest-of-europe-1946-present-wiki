Content Summary

Introduction — L’histoire d’un temps vivant. The volume opens against the 1867 dictum of the historian Jules Thiénot that history begins only when an epoch is dead; Bantigny reverses it to write the history of a living time. The book covers the period from 1981 (Mitterrand’s election) to “nos jours” and poses the question of what “fait époque” — of the tension between the old and the new, tradition and transformation. Its organising horizon is the “ère planétaire”: globalisation, the fading of frontiers, the dominance of technologies and networks. Three framing essays structure the introduction: Les frontières de l’ère planétaire (the nation questioned by a world that has become the best criterion for understanding the new era; France at once “pétrie de sa tradition” and “en retard sur l’horaire planétaire”); La modernité: avancées, détours et arrière-cours (modernity with its advances, detours and dark corners); and La fin des fins? (the end of ends — the exhaustion of teleological readings of history).

PREMIÈRE PARTIE — Politiques de crise et crises du politique

Chapitre I — Des roses et des épines

The first chapter opens the political narrative at the election of 10 May 1981 and the “état de grâce.” Sections: Les nouveaux maîtres du temps (the new masters of time — the Mitterrand presidency and its break with the previous period); Quels fers de lance pour la croissance? (the economic programme of the 1981–1983 moment, the nationalisations, the “relance” and its reversal); L’éducation à la « modernité » (education in the service of modernity).

Chapitre II — Les partages du pouvoir

The divisions and shared spaces of political power. Sections: La droite revient (the return of the right); Vague à l’âme pour gauche au pouvoir (the malaise of the left in power); D’une cohabitation à l’autre: alternances sans alternatives (from one cohabitation to the next — alternations without alternatives, the 1986, 1993 and 1997 cohabitations).

Chapitre III — Bouillons de ruptures

Breaks and ruptures in the political field. Sections: La norme des réformes (the norm of reform); Tensions et contestations (tensions and contestations — the 1995 “grève par procuration,” the sans-papiers movement, the dissolution of 1997); Le « sarkozysme » ou la droite « décomplexée » (Sarkozy’s presidency and the “uninhibited” right, 2007–2012).

Chapitre IV — Ambiguïtés sur l’échiquier

The ambiguities of the political chessboard. Sections: Passes d’armes à gauche (the internal duels of the left); Les droites et leur extrême (the rights and their extreme — the Front national, its rise and its electoral anchors); Politiques du triangle (the triangular political geometry of the three-way contest).

Chapitre V — La politique est leur affaire

Politics as the business of ordinary people. Sections: États de disgrâce (the discredit of the parties and the political class, abstention); Le vent se lève: nouvelles formes de politisation (new forms of political engagement — associations, mobilisations, alter-globalism); Les intellectuels et l’histoire au présent (intellectuals and history in the present).

DEUXIÈME PARTIE — La France du nouveau monde

Chapitre VI — Domination et contestations du « néolibéralisme »

The French encounter with neoliberalism. Sections: La loi des marchés (the law of the markets); L’impuissance de la puissance? (the powerlessness of power — the constraints of globalisation on the French state); Critiques de crise (critiques of the crisis — alter-globalism, ATTAC, the 2008 financial crisis).

Chapitre VII — Les équivoques de l’Europe

France and European construction, in all its ambiguities. Sections: Les faiseurs d’Europe (the makers of Europe); Morne plaine: enthousiasmes et désillusions (the changing French enthusiasm for and disenchantment with Europe, the Maastricht referendum of 1992, the 2005 referendum on the Constitutional Treaty); Turbulences et impuissances (the turbulence of the eurozone crisis).

Chapitre VIII — Au risque du monde

France in the world. Sections: Défaire Yalta? (the end of the Cold War order); Un nouvel ordre du monde (the new world order after 1989); Dans l’Occident désorienté (France in the disoriented West — the Gulf war, the wars of the post-9/11 era, France’s interventions and its place in the Atlantic alliance).

TROISIÈME PARTIE — Vivre ensemble?

Chapitre IX — Genres de vie

Ways of life and the intimate. Sections: La vie, l’amour, la mort (life, love, death — the demographic and family transformations); Hommes, femmes: nouveaux modes d’emploi (the recomposition of gender relations); Les usages de l’âge (the uses of age — youth, ageing, the generations).

Chapitre X — Travail: les mots et les maux

Work in the contemporary period. Sections: Le capitalisme des fragilités (the capitalism of fragilities — unemployment, precarity, the “nouveau monde économique”); Le sexe du travail (the gendered division of labour, the feminisation and segregation of work); De haute lutte: les intensités de la conflictualité (the intensities of social conflict — strikes and mobilisations).

Chapitre XI — La crise du creuset

The crisis of the French “creuset” (melting pot). Sections: L’ère du soupçon: haro sur l’immigration (the era of suspicion and the campaign against immigration); L’inégalité des chances (the inequality of opportunity, the banlieues, the “ghetto” and its representation); Nouveaux suspects d’altérité (new suspects of otherness — Islam and the laïcité debates, the foulard affairs, the “identité nationale” question).

Chapitre XII — Au miroir des territoires

France through the mirror of its territories. Sections: Espèces d’espaces: les bouleversements des rapports villes/campagnes (the upheaval of urban–rural relations); Lieux et non-lieux communs (places and non-places); Habiter: si loin, si près (housing, mobility, peri-urban France).

QUATRIÈME PARTIE — Imaginaires de l’ère planétaire

Chapitre XIII — Écrire le temps

The writing of time. Sections: L’écho du monde et les fils du quotidien (the echo of the world in everyday life); Temps incertains (uncertain times); Coups de théâtre: la scène et ses passages de témoins (the theatre of politics and its changes of guard).

Chapitre XIV — Regards-caméra

The gaze of the camera. Sections: Grand spectacle (the great spectacle); Le monde à bras-le-corps (the world grasped head-on); Cinémonde (French cinema and the world).

Chapitre XV — Mises en culture

The putting-into-culture of the era. Sections: Faire écrans (making screens — television and the audiovisual); Airs du temps (the airs of the time — music, the “variétés,” the “chanson”); Sport national (football and national sport — the 1998 World Cup, “Black-Blanc-Beur,” Zidane).

CONCLUSION — Faire histoire encore

Le changement: maintenant ou jamais? The choice of change. Faire société (making society). L’histoire faite monde (history become world). Le sacre du présent? (the consecration of the present). Sens de l’histoire (the sense of history). Un avenir pour le futur (a future for the future). The conclusion reflects on the meaning of writing contemporary history in the planetary era — the historian’s task, the question of the present, and the open future.

Relationship to the Wiki

Bantigny completes the French strand of the wiki. Where de Gaulle’s Discours et Messages (T2–T5) supply the first-person voice of the founder of the Fifth Republic (1946–1969), and Zancarini-Fournel’s La France du temps présent (1945–2005) gives the critical scholarly synthesis of reconstruction, the Algerian war, the Fifth Republic, May 1968 and the post-1973 crises, Bantigny carries the story forward from the 1981 alternance to the early twenty-first century. Her deliberately “planetary” framing — the era of globalisation, the “néolibéralisme,” the crisis of the creuset, gender and everyday life, and the cultural imagination — complements the Zancarini-Fournel volume as the French pillar of the wiki’s postwar continental coverage, read alongside the two Tusell volumes for Spain and the Italy volumes of Gentiloni Silveri and Mammarella.
