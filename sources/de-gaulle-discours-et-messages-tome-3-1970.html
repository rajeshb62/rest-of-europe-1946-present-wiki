Discours et Messages, Tome 3 : Avec le renouveau (1958-1962)

Charles de Gaulle — Discours et messages, Tome III : Avec le renouveau (Mai 1958–Juillet 1962). Librairie Plon, Paris, 1970; the present digital edition Plon / Édi8, 2014. The third of the five-tome collected edition of the speeches, messages and press conferences of General de Gaulle from 18 June 1940 to 28 April 1969, edited with the assistance of François Goguel; this volume covers the return of de Gaulle to power and the establishment of the Fifth Republic.

AuthorCharles de Gaulle (1890–1970) — French soldier and statesman, head of the Free French, head of the Provisional Government 1944–46, founder and first President of the Fifth Republic (1959–1969)
EditionLibrairie Plon, 1970; digital edition Plon, a department of Édi8, 2014; EAN numérique 9782259228497; text established with the collaboration of M. François Goguel
StructureNote de l’éditeur + note de l’éditeur pour le tome III + speeches, messages and press conferences arranged chronologically by year (1958–1962); 18 files in the EPUB, ~5340 lines / ~937,000 characters of extracted text
PeriodMay 1958 to July 1962 — the return of General de Gaulle to power (May 1958), the drafting and referendum on the Constitution of the Fifth Republic (autumn 1958), his presidency (elected December 1958, installed January 1959), the Algerian war and its winding-down, the decolonisation of the French Community in Africa, and the first phase of his foreign and defence policy
NoteA contemporary collection of de Gaulle’s own written and spoken texts, not a narrative history. The five-tome edition gathers only the speeches delivered in circumstances determined in advance whose exact text could be preserved — those written by his hand for the broadcast and televised allocations, or noted by official stenography for his public speeches and addresses before the Assemblies. The numerous improvised allocations whose text could not be reconstituted are excluded. François Goguel’s aide-mémoire and notes are set in the text. For this wiki it supplies, from the voice of the President of the Fifth Republic himself, the French case for the years 1958–1962

Contents. A chronological record of de Gaulle’s principal statements as he returned to power and founded the Fifth Republic. The volume opens with the Note de l’éditeur explaining the series, and the note for Tome III defining its scope as May 1958 to July 1962. The speeches of 1958 run from the return to power (the June allocation from the Palais d’Orsay and the address at Algiers), through the establishment of the new institutions — the speeches of the referendum campaign of autumn 1958, the adoption of the Constitution, the new President’s messages and the opening of the Community — to the first Conference de presse of the new regime. The year 1959 records the installation of the presidency, the presidential tour of Algeria, the inauguration of the Community’s institutions, de Gaulle’s state visit to Italy and his North African policy, the addresses of the Algerian self-determination offer, and his journey to the German Federal Republic. The year 1960 covers the Algerian policy of self-determination, the January week of the barricades, the Community’s transition to independence, de Gaulle’s reception of the heads of the new African states, his American tour (the address before Congress at Washington), and the continuing constitutional consolidation. The years 1961–1962 carry the Algerian crisis and its resolution — the April 1961 generals’ putsch and its address, the referendum on self-determination, the Évian negotiations — together with the development of the institutions, the visit of M. Khrushchev, and the closing speeches of 1962 up to the summer. The volume ends with the aide-mémoire chronologique and the Notes (235 numbered notes) prepared by François Goguel.

Character of the source. A first-person record of the founder of the Fifth Republic’s own words, kept as exact texts for the determined occasions and official addresses. It is not an analytic or reflective work but a corpus of state pronouncements — broadcast and televised allocations written by de Gaulle himself, and speeches and press conferences taken down by official stenography. Their character is that of acts of government and public declarations of policy, on Algeria, on the Community and decolonisation, on the constitution of the Fifth Republic, and on France’s place in the world. Goguel’s aide-mémoire, printed in bistre in the edition, situate each text in its circumstances.

Quality. Good. Born-digital EPUB of the Plon/Édi8 edition, extracted via OPF spine order with page markers (===== FILE n: ...). 1958

The return to power and the foundation of the Fifth Republic.

  • Conférence de presse tenue au Palais d’Orsay (June 1958) — the first great press conference of the return, in which de Gaulle presents himself as ready to assume the powers of the Republic and sets out his conception of the task.
  • Allocution prononcée à Alger — the speech at Algiers to the population and the army in the wake of the events of May 1958.
  • The radio and televised allocations from the Hôtel Matignon of June and July, and the allocation at the Préfecture de Police.
  • Discours devant l’Assemblée représentative de Madagascar, the discours prononcé à Tananarive and the discours prononcé à Dakar — the tour of the overseas territories at the moment of the referendum.
  • Discours prononcé place de la République à Paris — the great public address of the referendum campaign of September 1958.
  • Discours prononcé à Constantine (October 1958) — the Constantine programme of economic and social development for Algeria.
  • The referendum of 28 September 1958 and the adoption of the Constitution.
  • The speeches of November–December: the return to Paris, the message to the Parliament, the allocation to the Cité universitaire de Toulouse, and the first Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée.

1959

The installation of the presidency and the first full year of the Fifth Republic.

  • The New Year allocations; the allocation à l’École Polytechnique; the allocation à l’Hôtel de Ville de Paris and the allocation à la Sorbonne.
  • The state visit to Italy: the allocations at the Hôtel de Ville de Milan, at San Martino, at Solferino and the discours prononcé à Rome, au Capitole.
  • The Algerian policy: the tour of Tananarive and La Réunion, the discours d’ouverture de la session du Sénat de la Communauté, and the broadcast allocation of 16 September 1959 offering Algerian self-determination.
  • The reception of President Eisenhower at Le Bourget and the addresses of Paris.
  • The allocation à la Cour de Cassation and the allocation à l’École Militaire.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée (October 1959).
  • The African Community and the tour of Saint-Louis du Sénégal and the Assemblée fédérale du Mali; the message aux Armées and the message à la Métropole, à l’Algérie, à la Communauté.

1960

Self-determination for Algeria, the week of the barricades, and the Community’s passage to independence.

  • The January broadcast allocation at the time of the week of the barricades in Algiers; the allocation à l’École de l’Air de Salon-de-Provence.
  • The reception of M. Khrushchev: the allocation at Orly on his arrival.
  • The visit to Great Britain — the allocation au Guildhall de Londres and the discours prononcé à Westminster Hall.
  • The message to the Governor of Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon; the discours prononcé à Ottawa.
  • The American tour of 1960: the Conférence de presse au National Press Club de Washington, the discours devant les deux Chambres réunies en Congrès at Washington, and the allocations at New York, San Francisco and La Nouvelle-Orléans.
  • The African independence movement and the Community states.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée and the year’s closing allocations.

1961

The Algerian crisis: the generals’ putsch of April and the referendum on self-determination.

  • The January messages; the allocation à l’École Nationale d’Administration.
  • The allocation of 23 April 1961 in the face of the generals’ putsch; the measures of authority.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 11 April 1961 on Algeria and the nation.
  • The negotiations toward self-determination and the referendum of January 1961 on Algeria.
  • The agricultural and social allocations; the addresses to the armed forces.
  • The foreign policy of the year — the German question, the Atlantic alliance and the conception of a European Europe.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of 15 May 1962 (see under 1962 below for the sequence).

1962

The close of the Algerian war and the consolidation of the Fifth Republic’s institutions.

  • The ordre aux armées of 1 January 1962 and the New Year allocations.
  • The end of the Algerian war and the referendum of 8 April 1962 approving the Évian accords.
  • The visit to the provinces; the allocation at the Palais de l’Élysée on the reform of the institutions.
  • The referendum of 28 October 1962 on the election of the President of the Republic by universal suffrage.
  • The Conférence de presse tenue au Palais de l’Élysée of May 1962 and the great press conferences of the year.
  • The closing allocations of June and July 1962 (to 8 July) which end the volume.

Aide-mémoire chronologique et Notes

The volume closes with the aide-mémoire chronologique, situating each speech and message in the circumstances of the period, and with 235 numbered notes (prepared by François Goguel) which identify persons, places and events referred to in the texts — for example the press conference of 30 June 1955, the Anglo-American good offices after the bombardment of Sakkiet (February 1958), the decision of 6 May 1953 on the R.P.F., the referendum of 28 September 1958 (15.06% abstentions in the metropole, 79.2% Oui), the status of Algeria and its two electoral colleges under the Fourth Republic, the instructions to General Salan of 14 October, and the figures of the June–July 1958 loan (34 tonnes of gold in four days, 324 billion francs in the 17 June–17 July subscription).
