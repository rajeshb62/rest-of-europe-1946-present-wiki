France / Fifth Republic

  • Charles de Gaulle — Discours et Messages, Tome 2 : Dans l’attente (1946-1958) (1970) — the second volume of the collected edition of de Gaulle’s speeches, messages and press conferences, covering his withdrawal from power after January 1946, the campaign against the draft constitution of the Fourth Republic and the Bayeux declaration of June 1946, the foundation and campaigns of the R.P.F. (1947–1955), and the long “traversée du désert” to the eve of the return of May 1958; the General’s own voice on France under the Fourth Republic.
  • Charles de Gaulle — Discours et Messages, Tome 3 : Avec le renouveau (1958-1962) (1970) — the third volume of the collected edition of de Gaulle’s speeches, messages and press conferences, covering the return to power of May 1958, the drafting and adoption of the Constitution of the Fifth Republic (autumn 1958), his election and installation as President, the Algerian war and the first phase of decolonisation of the French Community, and the foreign and defence policy of 1959–1962; the President’s own voice on the founding of the Fifth Republic.
  • Charles de Gaulle — Discours et Messages, Tome 4 : Pour l’effort (1962-1965) (1970) — the fourth volume of the collected edition, covering the consolidation of the Fifth Republic and the October 1962 referendum on the election of the President by universal suffrage, the settlement of the Algerian question, the German tour and the Franco-German treaty of January 1963, the policy of national independence, the Latin-American tour of 1965 and the presidential re-election of December 1965; the President’s own voice on the middle years of the Fifth Republic.
  • Charles de Gaulle — Discours et Messages, Tome 5 : Vers le terme (1966-1969) (1970) — the fifth and final volume of the collected edition, covering the departure from the integrated command of NATO and the policy of national independence, the Moscow visit of 1966, the “live Quebec” affair of July 1967, the Polish, Romanian and Turkish visits, the crisis of May 1968 and the dissolution of the Assembly, and the retirement of General de Gaulle after the referendum of April 1969; the President’s own voice on the closing years of the Fifth Republic and the end of his presidency.

France / 1945 to the present — synthesis

  • Michelle Zancarini-Fournel — La France du temps présent (1945-2005) (2010) — the modern scholarly synthesis of the French “temps présent” in the Cornette Histoire de France, covering reconstruction (1944–1951), the Fourth Republic and the “transition modernisatrice”, the Algerian war, the founding of the Fifth Republic (1958–1968), May 1968 and its traces, and the crises, political responses and recompositions of 1975–2005; continuous social, cultural, economic and political history, complementary to (and in dialogue with) the de Gaulle Discours et Messages.
  • Ludivine Bantigny — La France à l’heure du monde. De 1981 à nos jours (2013) — volume 10 of the Histoire de la France contemporaine series, a synthesis of France from the 1981 Mitterrand election to the near-present written from a “planetary” angle: the Mitterrand years, the cohabitations and the Sarkozy presidency; globalisation, “néolibéralisme”, the ambiguities of Europe and France in the world; work, gender, the crisis of the “creuset” and the territories; and the cultural imagination of the “ère planétaire”.

Spain / Dictatorship and Transition

  • Javier Tusell — Historia de España en el siglo XX, III. La dictadura de Franco (2000) — the third volume of Tusell’s four-volume history, the continuous political, social and cultural history of the Franco dictatorship from 1 April 1939 to the dictator’s death in November 1975: the fascist temptation and wartime alignment (1939–1951), the years of consensus and economic opening (1951–1965), and the developmentalism, apertura and tardofranquismo of 1966–1975.
  • Javier Tusell — Historia de España en el siglo XX, IV. La transición democrática y el gobierno socialista (2000) — the fourth and final volume, covering the democratic Transition from Franco’s death (1975) — the third wave of democratisation, Juan Carlos, Arias, Suárez, the political reform law, the Constitution of 1978, the nationalisms, the 1979 elections, the fall of Suárez and 23-F, Calvo-Sotelo and the crisis of the centre, the PSOE victory of October 1982 — and the Socialist governments of Felipe González to the loss of the majority in 1989–1993.

Germany / Federal Republic (West Germany)

  • Hermann Weber (Hrsg.) — DDR, Dokumente zur Geschichte der Deutschen Demokratischen Republik 1945–1985 (1986) — a comprehensive source collection of 234 documents covering the whole history of the German Democratic Republic (dtv, München, Originalausgabe 1986; ISBN 3-423-02953-6; 476 pp.), edited by Hermann Weber, the leading Western scholar of GDR history (University of Mannheim). Eight chronological chapters each with an Einleitung and numbered documents: 1. Die Vorgeschichte der DDR 1945–1949 (Dok. 1–77 — the Soviet occupation zone, the SMAD, the SED fusion, land reform, the drift toward a separate state); 2. Stalinisierung der DDR 1949–1953 (Dok. 78–111 — the founding of the GDR, the Gleichschaltung and repression culminating in the uprising of 17 June 1953); 3. Der Ausbau des neuen Systems 1953–1961 (Dok. 112–146 — the post-Stalin “thaw”, Ulbricht against the inner-party opposition, the building of the Wall on 13 August 1961); 4. Die Festigung der DDR 1961–1965 (Dok. 147–161 — consolidation, the NÖS economic reforms); 5. Ulbrichts Modellversuche 1966–1970 (Dok. 162–180 — Ulbricht’s experiments and the Prague Spring crisis of 1968); 6. Anpassung an die UdSSR 1971–1975 (Dok. 181–205 — the change of leadership to Honecker and the closer alignment with the USSR that made the Ostpolitik détente with the Federal Republic possible); 7. Krisenhafte Entwicklung 1976–1980 (Dok. 206–216 — economic difficulty, dissent and emigration, the expatriation of Wolf Biermann 1976); 8. Probleme der DDR 1981–1985 (Dok. 217–234 — the X. Party Congress of April 1981, Honecker’s “Einheit von Wirtschafts- und Sozialpolitik”, the personality cult). Appendix: Zeittafel, Verzeichnis der Dokumente mit Quellennachweisen, Personenregister. The East-German counterpart to the West-German volumes in this section; built entirely from the local PDF (no further internet fetch).
  • II: Republik im Wandel 1974–1982, Die Ära Schmidt (1986) — the contemporary-history volume (Band 5/II) of the standard five-volume Geschichte der Bundesrepublik Deutschland (ed. Bracher, Eschenburg, Fest, Jäckel; Deutsche Verlags-Anstalt, Stuttgart / F. A. Brockhaus, Mannheim; set ISBN 978-3-421-06731-9), covering the era of Federal Chancellor Helmut Schmidt (1974–1982): Wolfgang Jäger’s Die Innenpolitik der sozial-liberalen Koalition 1974–1982 (pp. 9–274 — the first and second Schmidt/Genscher cabinets, economic crisis-management, the 1976 and 1980 Bundestag elections, the party system, terrorism at its height and the German Autumn, the nuclear-energy protest and the Greens, the trade unions and churches, the break of the SPD–FDP coalition and the change of government to Kohl in October 1982, and the domestic-political balance-sheet of the Schmidt era); Werner Link’s Außen- und Deutschlandpolitik in der Ära Schmidt 1974–1982 (pp. 275–432 — stabilisation and crisis-management, the European Monetary System and the European direct elections of 1979, the Ostpolitik in the phase of détente and relations with the Soviet Union, co-ordination difficulties with the USA, the “Europeanisation” through German–French co-operation, the German–German relations and the Uckermark summit of December 1981, and the worldwide dimension of UN policy and the Third World); and Joachim C. Fest’s closing essay Die deutsche Frage: Das offene Dilemma (pp. 433–446) plus Nachwort and Anhang (Zeittafel, Wahlen und Regierungen, Auswahlbibliographie, Anmerkungen, registers).
  • Heinrich August Winkler — Der lange Weg nach Westen, Band 2: Deutsche Geschichte vom «Dritten Reich» bis zur Wiedervereinigung (2000) — the second volume of Heinrich August Winkler’s acclaimed two-part German history (Verlag C.H. Beck, München, first ed. 2000; ISBN 3-406-49524-9 Band II). A “Problemgeschichte” centred on the relation of democracy and nation, and on the German “Sonderwege” and their ending: Einleitung (the author’s method and values); 1. Die deutsche Katastrophe: 1933–1945 (the Nazi seizure of power, the Gleichschaltung, the SS, war and the Holocaust); 2. Demokratie und Diktatur: 1945–1961 (the post-1945 German self-interrogation, occupation and division, the founding of the FRG and GDR, Adenauer’s Westintegration, the Wirtschaftswunder and rearmament, the Berlin Wall); 3. Zwei Staaten, eine Nation: 1961–1973 (the FRG under Erhard and the Grand Coalition, 1968, and Brandt’s neue Ostpolitik — the Moscow, Warsaw and Grundlagenvertrag treaties); 4. Annaherung und Entfremdung: 1973–1989 (the Schmidt era, economic crisis, terrorism and the German Autumn, the GDR’s Abgrenzung, the “Wende” of 1982, the German debates of the 1980s); 5. Einheit in Freiheit: 1989/90 (the collapse of the GDR, the fall of the Wall, the Two-plus-Four process, reunification); and the closing essay Abschied von den Sonderwegen: Rückblick und Ausblick plus Anhang (Abkürzungsverzeichnis, Anmerkungen, Personenregister).
  • Ilko-Sascha Kowalczuk — Die Übernahme: Wie Ostdeutschland Teil der Bundesrepublik wurde (2019) — an avowed essay (a Versuch) by the East German historian Ilko-Sascha Kowalczuk (Verlag C.H. Beck, München, first published August 2019, for the thirtieth anniversary of the fall of the Wall; ISBN 978-3-406-74020-6 Print, 978-3-406-74021-3 E-Book; 8,033 lines of extracted text), explaining “how our present has become historical”: the personal opening (Zwischen Aufbruch und Abbruch, framed by his father’s and his wife’s father’s biographies) and essay proper (Der Essay), then twenty-two topical chapters — Der letzte Republikgeburtstag (7 October 1989); Unmittelbare Folgen des Mauerfalls; Vom Runden Tisch zu freien Wahlen; Neue Erfahrung: Arbeitslosigkeit; Der Beitritt: alternativlos?; Was wäre wenn …?; In was für einer Verfassung?; Wer sind Ostdeutsche?; Ostdeutsche als «Andere»; Die Forderung: «Anpassung»; Die Angepassten; Die Hauptstadtdebatte; Stagnation; Die Treuhand; Die Eigentumsfrage; Die Landwirtschaft; Der ökonomische Kollaps als Kulturkampf; Das Unverständnis für Ostdeutschland; Das Beschäftigungsproblem; Die kulturelle Ausgrenzung; Mobilität als Problem; Rentenprobleme; Gewinnerinnen oder Verliererinnen?; Neue Benachteiligungen; Der Elitenaustausch an den Universitäten; Ost-Eliten im vereinigten Deutschland; Irgendwie alle! (the Academy of Arts fusion and Heiner Müller’s “Eroberung, Unterwerfung, nicht Vereinigung”); Gesellschaftspolitische Dimensionen der Aufarbeitung; Demokratie ohne Demokraten (87.5% of the GDR population without personal experience of a functioning democracy); Antisemitismus; Illiberale Einstellungen; Nationalismus; Rassismus; Medien in Ostdeutschland; Wahlen im Osten; and the closing Zwischen 1989: Hoffnungslosigkeit und 1990: Hoffnungen — plus Danksagung, Anmerkungen, Literaturhinweise, Personenregister. A critical East German view of the “Übernahme” (takeover) of the GDR by the Federal Republic and of the eastern German present — the natural post-reunification sequel to the Winkler and Weber volumes.

European Union / European Integration

  • Andrew Moravcsik — The Choice for Europe: Social Purpose and State Power from Messina to Maastricht (1998) — the foundational statement of the “liberal intergovernmentalist” theory of European integration (Cornell University Press, Ithaca, 1998; Routledge, London & New York, 1999; ISBN 0-8014-3509-9 Cornell, 1-85728-191-8 cased / 1-85728-192-6 paper Routledge; 22,790 lines of extracted text). Moravcsik argues that European integration is best explained as a series of rational choices by national leaders, responding to the economic interests of powerful domestic constituents (above all commercial interests), the relative power of states, and the role of international institutions in making commitments credible. He tests this account across the five most salient negotiations in EC history: the Treaty of Rome (1955–58), the consolidation of the customs union and the CAP (1958–69), the European Monetary System (1969–83), the Single European Act (1984–88), and the Maastricht Treaty on EMU (1988–91) — concluding that commercial and economic motivations dominated national preference formation, that bargaining outcomes reflected asymmetrical interdependence, and that institutional delegation (the pooling and delegation of sovereignty) served the purpose of credible commitment, while geopolitical threats, federalist ideas, technocratic imperatives and supranational entrepreneurship played a decidedly secondary role. Chapters: Introduction (The Choice for Europe); 1. Theorizing European Integration; 2. Finding the Thread: The Treaties of Rome, 1955–1958; 3. Grain and Grandeur: Consolidating the Common Market, 1958–1969; 4. Divergence and Convergence: Toward Monetary Integration, 1969–1983; 5. Relaunching Integration: The Single European Act, 1984–1988; 6. Economic and Monetary Union: Negotiating the Maastricht Treaty, 1988–1991; 7. European Integration in Retrospect. The cent… [truncated]
  • Mathieu Segers & Steven Van Hecke (eds.) — The Cambridge History of the European Union, Volume II: European Integration Inside-Out (2024) — the second volume of the two-volume Cambridge History of the European Union (Cambridge University Press, first published 2024; two-volume set ISBN 978-1-009-28437-0; Vol. II hardback ISBN 978-1-108-47893-9; DOI 10.1017/9781108781480; 33,406 lines of extracted text), examining the EU from an “inside-out” perspective — the internal developments that shaped the integration process. Organised thematically rather than as a strict timeline. Editorial “Reflections on the History and Historiography of European Integration” (Segers & Van Hecke) + three parts with 24 chapters by 25 contributors: Part I Milestones: Treaties and Treaty Changes (1. Early Forms of European Unity, Bossuat; 2. From Messina and Rome to the Single European Act, Gehler; 3. The Making of the European Union, Vanhoonacker; 4. From Maastricht and Copenhagen to Amsterdam and Nice, Dinan; 5. The Constitution Project, Lisbon and Beyond, Monar; 6. Moving beyond British Exceptionalism, Ludlow); Part II Instruments of Integration (7. In the Name of Social Stability: The European Payments Union, Steehouder; 8. Competition versus Planning, Warlouzet; 9. Commercial Banks, the Eurodollar Market and the Beginnings of Monetary Integration, Altamura; 10. From the Werner Report to the Start of EMU, Zimmermann; 11. The Euro Area Crisis, Verdun; 12. The Institutional and Legal Culture of European Integration, Bajon; 13. The Formation of the Migration Regime of the EU, Comte; 14. The Constitutional Dimension, Jakab & Kirchmair; 15. EU Enlargement: Origins and Practice, Maresceau); Part III Narratives and Outcomes (16. A Global Perspective on European Cooperation and Integration since 1918, Richard; 17. War, Peace and Memory: Franco-German Reconciliation, Germond; 18. The ‘Saints’ of European Integration, Gehler; 19. The EU and the Narrative of Prosperity, Hiepel; 20. Changing Europe’s Economic History, Berend; 21. The EU and the Narrative of Solidarity, Ross; 22. European Solidarity: The Difficult Art of Managing Interdependence, Crespy & Verschueren; 23. Ideologies of EU Democracy since 1950, Sternberg; 24. Democratic Challenges since Maastricht, Wolfs). The editors stress that every chapter builds on original multinational and/or transnational archival research, stretching beyond “silo studies” toward a richer, more comprehensive, interdisciplinary picture.

Italy / Republic

  • Giuseppe Mammarella — L’Italia contemporanea (1943-1998) (2000) — a classic single-volume scholarly history of Italy in the “Le vie della civiltà” series, from the fall of fascism and the war (1943–1948), through the centrismo (1948–1958), the centro-sinistra (1958–1974), the end of centro-sinistra and the 1980s, to “verso la seconda Repubblica” (1989–1998): the elections of 1948, the “legge truffa” of 1953, the “apertura a sinistra”, the strategy of tension and the “hot autumn”, the “compromesso storico”, the 1976 elections, the return to the Cold War, the fateful 1989, the annus horribilis of 1992, Tangentopoli, and the governments of the left.
  • Umberto Gentiloni Silveri — Storia dell’Italia contemporanea (1943-2019) — a modern scholarly synthesis of contemporary Italian history from the summer of 1943 as a “theatre of war” to 2019: the fall of fascism, democracy, the referendum and the Constitution, the reasons of the “miracle”, the anni Settanta and the “funerale della Repubblica” (the Moro kidnapping), the beginning of the end, the fall of the Wall and Tangentopoli, the “transizione mancata” and the imperfect bipolarism, the eurozone, and Italy between rinascita and declino to the present.
  • Nico Perrone — Enrico Mattei (2012) — compact Il Mulino biography of Enrico Mattei (1906–1962): Agip after the Liberation, the founding of Eni/Snam (1953), Italian energy autonomy and oil diplomacy (Mediterranean, Africa, Soviet crude), Italo–American friction over hydrocarbons, and Mattei’s death in the Bascapè air disaster (27 October 1962); essential for the economic miracle and state enterprise under centrismo.

USSR / Communist Party of the Soviet Union

  • История СССР с древнейших времен до наших дней, Том 11: Советский Союз на пути к развитому социализму (1945–1961) (1980) — Volume 11 of the twelve-volume academic История СССР (Наука, Москва, 1980), edited by И. Б. Берхин, covering the Soviet Union from the end of the Second World War through the fourth five-year plan and postwar reconstruction (1945–1950) and the transformation of the 1950s, to the XXI and XXII Congresses and the 1961 Programme for building communism (1945–1961).
  • История КПСС (History of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union), 6th supplemented edition (1982) — the official party-history textbook by an authorial collective led by академик Б. Н. Пономарев (Политиздат, Москва, 1982; 784 pp.), a single-volume narrative from the origins of Russian Marxism (1883, Plekhanov’s Liberation of Labour group) through the XXVI Party Congress (23 February – 3 March 1981). Chapters in this wiki’s span (XVI–XX, 1945–1981): postwar reconstruction and the formation of the world socialist system (XVI), developed socialism and the Congresses from XIX to XXII (XVII), the leading role of the party and the socialist community 1962–1970 (XVIII), developed socialist society and the Programme of Peace 1971–1980 (XIX), and the XXVI Congress (XX).
  • Iosif Vissarionovich Stalin: A Short Biography (2nd ed., 1948) — the official short biography of Stalin (Институт Маркса — Энгельса — Ленина при ЦК ВКП(б), OGIZ — State Publishing House of Political Literature, Moscow, 1948), from his birth in Gori (1879) through the Great Patriotic War and postwar reconstruction; its final chapter (XI) carries the narrative into 1946, the post-war reconstruction and the elections to the Supreme Soviet of February 1946.
  • История внешней политики СССР 1917–1980, Том второй (1945–1980 гг.) (1981) — Volume Two of the two-volume official history of Soviet foreign policy, edited by А. А. Громыко and Б. Н. Пономарев (Наука, Москва, 4th rev. ed., 1981; Institute of History of the USSR), covering Soviet foreign policy from the end of the Second World War and the postwar settlement (Ch. XIV) through the German question and the division of Germany, the Korean and Indochinese wars, disarmament and the nuclear test ban, the Hungarian events and the Suez crisis, decolonisation and aid to the peoples of Asia and Africa, relations with the capitalist states and the policy of détente, and the Peace Programmes of the XXIV and XXV Party Congresses to the close of the 1970s (Ch. XXXII, 1976–1980).

USSR / Post-Soviet Russia

  • Пихоя Р. Г. — Москва. Кремль. Власть. Две истории одной страны. Россия на изломе тысячелетий (1985–2005) (2007) — Rudolf Pikhoya’s political history of Soviet and Russian power, 1985–2005, in two parts: «Закат империи» (The Decline of the Empire), covering Gorbachev’s accession, «ускорение» and perestroika, the Chernobyl disaster, glasnost and managed democratisation, the rise of the nationality question and Nagorny Karabakh, the elections of the USSR people’s deputies, the Russian factor and the Declaration of State Sovereignty, the “war of laws”, the referendum and Novo-Ogaryovo, the August putsch and the collapse of the CPSU, and the analysis of why the Soviet Union fell apart (1985–1991); and «Россия в поисках себя» (Russia in Search of Itself), covering the market reforms, the political-constitutional crisis of 1992–1993 and the new Constitution, the “tired” Russia of 1994–1999, the default of 1998, and the building of the vertical of power under Putin to 2005.
  • Юрчак А. — Это было навсегда, пока не кончилось. Последнее советское поколение (2014) — Alexei Yurchak’s anthropological study of the last Soviet generation (НЛО, Москва, 2014; Princeton University Press original 2005), an analysis of the system of late socialism (mid-1950s to mid-1980s, including the gap years 1980–1985) through the performative shift of the Soviet authoritative discourse and the practice of «вненаходимость» (vnenakhodimost, being simultaneously inside and outside the discursive field of the system): the hegemony of form, ideology turned inside out, the “imagined West”, Western rock and «Разноцветный коммунизм», and the irony of vnenakhodimost (necro-aesthetics, stiob, the anecdote); its Заключение carries the argument through perestroika to the collapse of the Soviet system (1985–1991).
  • Мединский В. Р., Торкунов А. В. — История России. 1945 год — начало XXI века. 11 класс (2024) — the official Russian school textbook (Просвещение, Москва, 3-е изд., обновл., 2024), a survey of the history of the USSR and the Russian Federation from 1945 to the early 2020s: postwar reconstruction and the early Cold War (1945–1953), the Khrushchev thaw (1953–1964), the Brezhnev period of “developed socialism” (1964–1985), perestroika and the collapse of the USSR (1985–1991), and the Russian Federation from 1992 through the 1990s, the Putin era, and the events of the early 2020s including the special military operation (СВО).
